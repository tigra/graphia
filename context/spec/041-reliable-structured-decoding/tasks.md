## Slicing rationale (read before reordering)

**Why the instrumentation fix is Slice 1, ahead of the swap itself.** `SchemaStats["Diary"].failure_rate` is 1.0 today by construction, so **the ADR-013 transport gate cannot observe its own fix**. Measuring before repairing the instrument would produce a number nobody can trust in either direction.

**Why the client swap and the method-pinning are two slices.** Measured: `method="json_schema"` is already `ChatOllama`'s default. So swapping the client *alone* delivers reliable decoding and is independently verifiable on a live model. Pinning the method at the provider boundary is then a separate, defensive increment whose value is cloud-parity proof and immunity to a third-party default flip — not correctness.

**Why the withdrawal comes after the swap, not before.** Withdrawing the recovery first would reintroduce the 46% fallback for the duration of a slice. After the swap the recovery is provably unreachable, so removing it regresses nothing.

**Why counting comes before marking.** The counts are a record-level number with no data-shape change; the marks require a carrier on three separate channels. Counting first means every later slice has a number to check itself against.

**Why the three marking slices are separate.** Each is end-to-end (carrier → emission → tokenizer → style) and independently visible. The thought channel is isolated deliberately: it is the only piece requiring a **data-shape change to another spec's channel**, and it is the one whose blast radius was estimated rather than measured. It can be pulled out of this spec without disturbing its neighbours.
